Expired-patent screening: non-powered household cleaning tools

Run run-real-5, 2026-11-07. US patents only.

Research screening only. Not a legal opinion or freedom-to-operate determination.

1. Executive summary

The run looked at 2404 US utility patents in the cleaning-tool CPC groups. 299 of them appear in Official Gazette expiration notices from the last 104 weeks, 209 passed the non-powered screen and 8 were analysed. Recommended for technical and legal review: US 8,800,092 (Apparatus to remove a disposable cloth from a hand operated flat mop through a single trigger mechanism without having to touch the disposable cloth), US 8,826,487 (Mop cleaning set). Every status is dated and comes from public notices; none of this is legal clearance.

2. Search scope and methodology

Candidates came from the BigQuery PatentsView tables, filtered to core cleaning-tool CPC groups. Their patent numbers were matched against 104 weeks of Official Gazette expiration notices. Patents with a powered co-classification or a power source in an independent claim were removed. Every later reinstatement list was scanned before a patent was called expired, and confidence was set by rule from the number of sources and the completeness of that scan. 8 candidates were summarised and scored.

  • Search: CPC prefixes for non-powered household cleaning tools (A47L 13, A47L 1, A47L 17, A47K 11/10 and related groups).
  • Status source: Official Gazette notices, 104 weekly issues fetched out of 104 requested.
  • Screening: powered co-classes removed, power terms in independent claims removed, unclear cases flagged.
  • Status rules: a later official notice wins; expired means listed as expired with no later reinstatement found.
  • Scoring: commercial relevance 35%, engineering effort 20% (inverted), evidence quality 25%, unresolved risk 20% (inverted).

US 10,052,665: Devices and methods for dispensing fluids and wiping surfaces

The patent describes a cleaning device designed to dispense fluid onto a surface and then wipe it off. The device includes a housing with an outer and inner portion, a dispenser with a porous material for delivering fluid, and a wiper with a stiff inner core and an outer fabric layer. The wiper can be repositioned to use different parts of the fabric. The device can also include caps for the dispenser and wiper. The dispenser and wiper have specific dimensions for their protruding portions. Another embodiment describes a housing with separate cavities for the dispenser and wiper, with the wiper core being slightly smaller than its cavity to allow for easy positioning.

Mechanism: The cleaning device works by having a dispenser that selectively delivers fluid to a surface being cleaned and a wiper that wipes the dispensed fluid from the surface. The wiper can be repositioned to use different portions of the outer fabric layer.

  • Claim element: A cleaning device configured to selectively dispense a fluid to a surface being cleaned, the cleaning device comprising: a housing comprising an outer housing portion and an inner housing portion, the outer housing portion configured to be grasped and manipulated by a user during a cleaning procedure, the outer housing portion comprising a wiper portion and an inner cavity, the inner housing portion comprising a dispenser cavity; wherein at least a portion of the inner housing portion is positioned within the inner cavity of the outer housing portion; a dispenser comprising a porous or fluid permeable material, the dispenser positioned at least partially within the dispenser cavity of the inner housing portion and protruding at least partially from an opening of the dispenser cavity; wherein the dispenser comprises a dispensing surface configured to contact a surface being cleaned and to selectively deliver fluid thereto; and a wiper removably attached to the wiper portion of the outer housing portion, the wiper comprising an inner core and an outer fabric layer, the inner core comprising a material that is stiffer than the outer fabric layer, a portion of the outer fabric layer comprising a wiping surface, the wiping surface configured to wipe dispensed fluid from a surface being cleaned, wherein the wiper is repositionable relative to the wiper portion of the outer housing portion to enable a different portion of the outer fabric layer to comprise the wiping surface.
  • Claim element: A cleaning device configured to selectively dispense a fluid to a surface being cleaned, the cleaning device comprising: a housing comprising a first end and a second end, the first end comprising a dispenser cavity and the second end comprising a wiper cavity; a dispenser positioned at least partially within the dispenser cavity, the dispenser comprising a porous or fluid permeable material; wherein the dispenser comprises a dispensing portion at least partially protruding from the dispenser cavity, the dispensing portion configured to contact a surface being cleaned and to selectively deliver fluid thereto; and a wiper removably positioned at least partially within the wiper cavity, the wiper comprising a wiper core wrapped in at least one layer of fabric; wherein the wiper core and the wiper cavity each comprise a thickness, and the thickness of the wiper cavity is greater than the thickness of the wiper core.
  • Unknown: The claims do not specify if the device is powered or non-powered.

US 10,065,611: Windshield cleaning implement

The patent describes a windshield cleaning implement with an arcuate, flexible shaft and a cleaning head that can simultaneously hold a sponge, a scrubbing brush, and a wiper blade. These elements are removably attached to the cleaning head via tubular slots, allowing the user to dampen, scrub, and scrape a windshield in one motion. The device is operated manually via a handle at the proximal end of the shaft.

Mechanism: The windshield cleaning implement has an arcuate, flexible shaft with a cleaning head at its distal end. The cleaning head features multiple tubular slots on its front face, allowing for the removable attachment of a sponge, a wiper blade, and a scrubbing brush. The sponge, scrubbing brush, and wiper blade can be simultaneously used to dampen, scrub, and scrape a windshield with a single swipe. A handle at the proximal end of the shaft enables the user to apply force, pressing the attached cleaning elements against the windshield.

  • Claim element: an elongated shaft having a proximal end and a distal end, wherein said shaft is elongated, arcuate and constructed with a resilient but flexible material
  • Claim element: a cleaning head attached to the distal end of said shaft, said cleaning head having a front face and a rear face
  • Claim element: said cleaning head including a plurality of juxtaposed, tubular slots on the front face
  • Claim element: a sponge having a projection that is slidably receivable within one of said tubular slots to removably attach said sponge to the front face of said cleaning head
  • Claim element: a wiper blade having a projection that is slidably receivable within one of said tubular slots to removably attach said wiper blade to the front face of said cleaning head
  • Claim element: a scrubbing brush having a projection that is slidably receivable within one of said tubular slots to removably attach said scrubbing brush to the front face of said cleaning head, between said sponge and said wiper blade
  • Claim element: a handle at the proximal end of said shaft whereby, when a user applies a predetermined amount of force to the handle, said sponge, said scrubbing brush and said wiper blade are simultaneously forced against a windshield
  • Unknown: unclear

8. Recommended shortlist and rationale

US 8,800,092: Apparatus to remove a disposable cloth from a hand operated flat mop through a single trigger mechanism without having to touch the disposable cloth

The patent describes a flat mop with a single trigger mechanism to remove disposable cloths without touch, which aligns directly with the intended product category of manual cleaning tools. The expiration due to non-payment places the technology in the public domain, eliminating licensing barriers. However, the mechanism involves molded steel pins and cover members, requiring specific tooling.

US 8,826,487: Mop cleaning set

Direct category match for manual mop tools. Expired status eliminates licensing barriers. Mechanical complexity is moderate.

9. Engineering validation and specialist-review next steps

For US 8,800,092, check whether the trigger mechanism is manual or powered and verify the tooling for the molded steel pins and cover members before product work. For US 8,826,487, check the related active continuation and clarify whether the mop cleaning set is powered before product work.

  • Confirm each status in USPTO Patent Center and check for a pending petition.
  • Read the full claims and drawings; build a test sample before tooling.
  • Have a patent attorney run a freedom-to-operate search, including related patents.
  • Check design patents and trade dress that may cover the product's look.

10. Limitations, assumptions and references

Research screening only. Not a legal opinion or freedom-to-operate determination.

Limitations

  • US patents only; nothing here speaks to other countries.
  • Screening only. Related patents, design patents and trade dress may still cover a product.
  • A lapsed patent can be reinstated later; intervening rights protect only use that began during the lapse.
  • Terminal disclaimers are checked only where PatentsView records them.

Key assumptions

  • The Gazette scan is current to the issue of 2026-11-03; expirations appear about eight weeks after the anniversary.
  • PatentsView bibliographic data ends with grants of 2021-09-28 and claims with grants of October 2019.
  • Patent term adjustment is not available in public tables, so end-of-term dates are estimates.

Quality of information

  • Official Gazette notices are the USPTO's own publication (grade A).
  • EPO OPS cross-check: used
  • 199 eligible candidates were not analysed because of the analysis cap.
