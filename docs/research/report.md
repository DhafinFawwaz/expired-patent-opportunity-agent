**Bottom line.** Build the agent as a single-thread, deterministic LangGraph 1.2.x *workflow* whose only live, keyless source of maintenance-fee status is the **USPTO Official Gazette weekly notices**: both the "Notice of Expiration of Patents Due to Failure to Pay Maintenance Fee" and "Patents Reinstated Due to the Acceptance of a Late Maintenance Fee" lists are public HTML with no login and no robots.txt, and a deep archive at `www.uspto.gov/web/offices/com/sol/og/<YYYY>/week<NN>/TOC.htm` was verified back to **2008**, removing the 52-week limit of `patentsgazette.uspto.gov` ([OG notices 2025 index](https://www.uspto.gov/learning-and-resources/official-gazette/official-gazette-notices-2025); KLS). The cross-check is the **EPO OPS legal service (INPADOC)**, free up to 4 GB/week after a web registration that involves no identity documents (the user's registration is pending activation), using US codes `LAPS`/`STCH`/`FP` for lapse and `PRDP` for reinstatement ([EPO fair use charter](https://www.epo.org/en/service-support/ordering/fair-use); KLS). **Google BigQuery `patents-public-data`** supplies discovery (CPC on `patents.publications`, grants current to 2026-09-15), claims text, family/continuity/citation links and filing/priority dates, but carries no live legal status; its PEDS snapshot ends **2024-10-12** and is a historical baseline and fixture source only ([BigQuery public datasets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/public-data); BQ). The USPTO ODP `ptmnfee2` MaintFeeEvents file is the richest status source but every `api.uspto.gov/api/v1/*` path returned **403 without a key**, the legacy `bulkdata.uspto.gov` host no longer resolves, and the key path runs through ID.me, so it is shipped only as a documented, fixture-tested adapter marked "requires key" ([MaintFeeEvents documentation via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20241210103618id_/https://bulkdata.uspto.gov/data/patent/maintenancefee/MaintFeeEventsFileDocumentation.doc); KLS). The LLM, reached through the user's `freellm` router as `ChatLiteLLMRouter`, is confined to about five bounded structured-output calls (plan, query expansion, per-candidate technical summary with mandatory evidence IDs, opportunity rationale, report prose); everything else, including every data-source call, normalisation, status rules, scoring, generation and validation, is plain Python, which is what makes "the workflow must actually execute tools" demonstrable and what survives weak free models ([Anthropic, Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents); LG). The two human gates (Approval, Human review) are `interrupt()` + `Command(resume=...)` on a `SqliteSaver` checkpointer; the terminal is Rich + Typer; the evidence ledger is a first-class state key; the `.docx` and `.pptx` are rendered by python-docx 1.2.0 and python-pptx 1.0.2 from one validated Pydantic `ReportBundle` and then re-opened and diffed against it ([LangGraph interrupts docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/interrupts); LG; DOC). The live conflicting-evidence case for the demo already exists: **US 8,646,606** is listed in 1552 OG 61 (2026-11-03) as reinstated on 2026-10-07, while Google Patents (INPADOC) still shows "Expired - Fee Related" with no `PRDP` event and the BigQuery PEDS snapshot still says "Patented Case" ([Google Patents US8646606B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8646606B2/en); KLS; BQ).

## Gazette notices are the only keyless live status feed, and three other sources fill distinct roles

The brief needs four kinds of data: candidate discovery, status evidence (expiry and reinstatement), related-patent records, and term data. No single keyless source gives all four, and the notes converge on a layered design in which each source has one job and its freshness is stated on every record.

**Status evidence: Official Gazette notices (primary).** The weekly OG notice page is plain HTML with `<pre>` blocks; `https://patentsgazette.uspto.gov/robots.txt` returns 404 and `week44/OG/TOC.htm` returned 200 without credentials on 2026-11-07 ([OG week 44](https://patentsgazette.uspto.gov/week44/OG/TOC.htm); KLS). Expiration rows sit under headings of the form "PATENTS WHICH EXPIRED ON <date>" and carry patent number, application number and issue date; reinstated rows carry the petition "Granted Date"; neither carries titles ([reference 12](https://patentsgazette.uspto.gov/week44/OG/TOC.htm); README; KLS). The deep archive at `www.uspto.gov/web/offices/com/sol/og/<YYYY>/week<NN>/TOC.htm` returned 200 with both headings present for sampled weeks in **2008, 2010, 2012, 2015, 2020, 2024 and 2026**, each list entirely on the week's single page (62-131 `<pre>` blocks), with **7,219 rows** in 2026 week 11 and **2,140 rows** in 2015 week 40 ([OG notices 2025 index](https://www.uspto.gov/learning-and-resources/official-gazette/official-gazette-notices-2025); KLS). Two parsing pitfalls are verified: older issues print the issue date as `MM/DD/YY` while 2024-2026 issues use `MM/DD/YYYY`, and reissues appear as `RE.46,721` / `RE. 47,917`; `TOC.htm` returns 404 for 2002, 2003, 2005, 2006 and 2007, which use `week40/patcont.htm` → `ogYYYYWW.htm` plus a zip instead (KLS). The listing lag is about **8 weeks** in the live 2026-11-03 issue (expiries of 2026-09-04/06/09) versus "approximately 3 months" in the archived eOG:P FAQ ([eOG:P FAQ via UNT archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120928001959/http://www.uspto.gov/news/og/patent_og/eogfaq.jsp); KLS). USPTO's terms of use (updated 2026-08-13) say the website is "not designed or intended to be a source for bulk downloads" and that "Public domain information may be freely distributed and copied"; the notes read one cached request per week-page with a descriptive User-Agent as consistent with that ([USPTO terms of use](https://www.uspto.gov/terms-use-uspto-websites); KLS). The OG bulk archive `gzlst` on the ODP returned `{"message":"Unauthorized"}` without a key, and MPEP 2575's "annual compilation" of expirations could not be located online ([MPEP 2575](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2575.html); KLS). No reusable OG parser exists: GitHub code search for `"Patents Reinstated"` found only VestraIntel's toy parser (hard-coded `week02`, discards application numbers and dates, never reads the reinstated list) and `patent-yard`'s unlicensed 186-line stdlib parser, whose TOC-anchor sectioning, `PATENTS WHICH EXPIRED ON` block split, `RE\d{2},\d{3}` regex alternative, 358-day cache-staleness rule and 429-aware backoff are the reusable ideas ([patent-yard pipeline/uspto.py](https://github.com/SanjayK57/patent-yard/blob/main/pipeline/uspto.py); GH).

**Status evidence: EPO OPS legal service (cross-check).** Anonymous access does not exist: `GET https://ops.epo.org/3.2/rest-services/legal/publication/docdb/US8646606` returned **HTTP 403** "violation of Fair Use policy" on 2026-11-07, and the reference guide states "Anonymous users have no access to OPS" ([OPS v3.2 reference guide](https://link.epo.org/web/ops_v3.2_documentation_-_version_1.3.19_en.pdf); KLS). Registered non-paying users get **4 GB per week** free; the 2018 guide revision only removed the printed number and defers to the fair-use charter, and the FAQ confirms the quota rose from 2.5 GB to 4 GB in 2018 ([EPO FAQ](https://epo.org/en/service-support/faq/searching-patents/open-patent-services/ops-v32/why-registration-needed-ops-32); KLS). Nothing in the registration path requires government ID; only paying users go through "customer validation", and no EPO document states the activation turnaround ([OPS terms PDF v2.0](https://developers.epo.org/sites/default/files/terms_and_conditions_OPS%202.0%20EN_DE_FR.pdf); KLS). Authentication is OAuth2 client-credentials; every response carries `X-Throttling-Control` (per-service traffic light over a 60-second window, `inpadoc=green:60` when idle, 30 when overloaded) plus `X-IndividualQuotaPerHour-Used` and `X-RegisteredQuotaPerWeek-Used`, with a global ~450 MB/hour cap and a 403 + `X-Rejection-Reason` on exhaustion ([OPS reference guide §2.3.3](https://link.epo.org/web/ops_v3.2_documentation_-_version_1.3.19_en.pdf); KLS). One status check is one `/legal/publication/docdb/US<number>` call, so a five-candidate run is negligible against the quota (KLS). The client is `python-epo-ops-client` 4.2.3 (Apache-2.0, now under the `ip-tools` org, Python 3.6-3.15 classifiers), whose `legal()` service landed in 4.2.0 on 2025-07-26 and which handles token renewal and header-driven throttling but leaves XML parsing to the caller ([CHANGES.md](https://github.com/ip-tools/python-epo-ops-client/blob/main/CHANGES.md); GH; KLS). Its one caveat for this stack: it is `requests`-based and synchronous, so wrap it behind the adapter interface or call it via a thread (GH). INPADOC's reinstatement feed lags: the newest US `PRDP` gazette date in the W45/2026 coverage file is **2026-09-28**, and 8,646,606 (reinstated 2026-10-07 per OG) shows no `PRDP` as of 2026-11-07 ([INPADOC coverage XLSX](https://link.epo.org/web/coverage/weekly/EN-Contents-coverage-INPADOC-legal-event-data.xlsx); KLS). Until credentials arrive, the only keyless cross-check is a manual, documented look at Google Patents or Espacenet for a handful of candidates (KLS).

**Status evidence: ODP MaintFeeEvents (`ptmnfee2`), key-gated.** The dataset is "Patent Maintenance Fee Events (1981 - Present)", a weekly cumulative `MaintFeeEvents.zip` ([catalog.data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/patent-grant-maintenance-fee-events-september-1-1981-present-7d26f); KLS). Live checks on 2026-11-07: `data.uspto.gov/bulkdata/datasets/ptmnfee2` renders a 200 Angular shell behind AWS WAF; `api.uspto.gov/api/v1/datasets/products/ptmnfee2`, the two file-path variants, the `gzlst` product, `patent/applications/search` and the `api-docs` paths all return **403**; `bulkdata.uspto.gov` fails DNS ([glama listing for pipeworx](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/u3umt7mmlu); KLS; GH). The SPA bundle contains no `x-api-key`, `api.uspto.gov` or `datasets/products/files` strings, so whether the website's own download button serves the zip anonymously is **unverified** (KLS). The record layout (June 2018 documentation, recovered from Wayback) is 59 characters: patent number cols 1-13 (reissues `RE` + 5 digits), application number 15-22, entity 24 (`Y`/`M`/`N`), filing date 26-33, grant date 35-42, event date 44-51, event code 53-57, CRLF ([MaintFeeEvents documentation via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20241210103618id_/https://bulkdata.uspto.gov/data/patent/maintenancefee/MaintFeeEventsFileDocumentation.doc); KLS). The key path, per a third-party guide that matches USPTO's 2025 bulletin wording, is USPTO.gov account → ID.me linking at `data.uspto.gov/myodp` → key shown on dashboard, and "USPTO does not provide a way to change your API key" ([API_KEY_GUIDE.md](https://github.com/john-walkoe/uspto_fpd_mcp/blob/main/API_KEY_GUIDE.md); GH; KLS). A Wayback snapshot of the zip from **2025-03-07** answers HTTP 200 with `content-length: 179972240`, usable only as a labelled historical fixture source, never as current status ([Wayback MaintFeeEvents.zip](https://web.archive.org/web/20250307054347/https://bulkdata.uspto.gov/data/patent/maintenancefee/MaintFeeEvents.zip); GH). Every maintained Python USPTO library in 2026 (`pyUSPTO` 0.6.1, `uspto-odp` 1.0.1, `patent-client-agents` 0.32.0) assumes this key, and `patent_client` 5.0.19 is archived with dead PEDS/PTAB backends, so none is a dependency ([pyUSPTO README](https://github.com/DunlapCoddingPC/pyUSPTO/blob/main/README.md); GH).

**Discovery, claims, related patents and term dates: BigQuery `patents-public-data`.** Twenty-two datasets exist; only `patents` (IFI CLAIMS, **173,516,400 rows**, 2,952 GiB, last loaded 2026-09-23 with grants to 2026-09-15), `google_patents_research` (same date) and `cpc` (2026-06-01) are current ([project page](https://console.cloud.google.com/bigquery?p=patents-public-data); BQ). `patents.publications` has `cpc[]`, `uspc[]`, `family_id`, `priority_claim[]`, `parent[]`/`child[]` (the relation is in `type`, not `category`, with values `continuation` 1,135,253, `division` 310,745, `continuation-in-part` 244,041, `a-371-of-international` 131,355 over US B1/B2 2010-2025), `citation[]`, `claims_localized[]`, `entity_status` (`large` 3,821,657 / `small` 898,048 / `micro` 97,619) and INTEGER `YYYYMMDD` dates, but **no maintenance, lapse, reinstatement, legal-status, terminal-disclaimer, PTA or term column of any kind** ([Google Patents Public Datasets](https://console.cloud.google.com/marketplace/product/google_patents_public_datasets/google-patents-public-data); BQ; DOM). Maintenance-fee-eligible US grants are `country_code='US' AND kind_code IN ('B1','B2','E1')`; `application_kind` is unreliable (243 B2 rows carry `D`) (BQ). The table is unclustered, so a `WHERE` clause never reduces bytes: CPC+title discovery dry-runs at **35.73 GB**, `claims_localized` at **127.73 GB**, `abstract_localized` at 216.71 GB and `description_localized` at **1,157.57 GB**, above the whole monthly free tier; `google_patents_research.publications` gives `title` for 12.06 GB and `cited_by` (forward citations) for 23.74 GB ([BigQuery cost best practices](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/best-practices-costs); BQ). Fetch claims for the whole candidate set in one `IN UNNEST(@list)` query, never per patent (BQ). The sandbox gives 1 TiB of queries per month, a lifetime 10 GiB of storage and 60-day table expiry, so results belong in `fixtures/`/`outputs/`, not in BigQuery tables ([BigQuery sandbox docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/sandbox); BQ). Access from Python is `google-cloud-bigquery` 3.47.0 with ADC, an explicit `project=`, `QueryJobConfig(dry_run=True, use_query_cache=False)` to read `total_bytes_processed`, then `maximum_bytes_billed` on the real run ([QueryJobConfig reference](https://docs.cloud.google.com/python/docs/reference/bigquery/latest/google.cloud.bigquery.job.QueryJobConfig); BQ; GH). For status, BigQuery is strictly historical: `uspto_peds.backfile`/`frontfile` (October 2024 PEDS JSON) carry `applicationStatusCategory` "Patent Expired Due to NonPayment of Maintenance Fees Under 37 CFR 1.362" (**2,712,209** backfile rows) and a newest-first `prosecutionHistoryData` JSON string with `EXP.`, `REM.`, `REIN`, `M1551/2/3`, `M2551/2/3`, `M3551/2/3`, `PMFP/PMFG/PMFD`, `DIST`, `P574`, but a status-only lookup costs 2.62/1.64 GB and touching the history column costs ~150-190 GB; `uspto_oce_pair` ends 2016-03 (status 250 expired, 153 reinstated); `patentsview` ends with grants of 2021-09-28 and its `us_term_of_grant.lapse_of_patent` is the string "NULL" in every row ([PatentsView forum](https://patentsview.org/forum/8/topic/275); BQ). PEDS itself was retired on 2025-03-14, so the BigQuery copy will never refresh ([USPTO PEDS retirement](https://www.uspto.gov/system-status/20250212-patent-examination-data-system-peds-retirement); BQ). Term data: filing, priority and `priority_claim[].filing_date` are complete and fresh; **PTA days exist in no public table** (`uspto_oce_pair.patent_term_adjustments` does not exist; PEDS has only `PTAC` marker events; `patentsview.us_term_of_grant.term_extension` is an undocumented integer), so end-of-term must be computed as 20 years from the earliest US non-provisional filing with PTA flagged unknown unless read from the front page ([USPTO PatEx page](https://www.uspto.gov/ip-policy/economic-research/research-datasets/patent-examination-research-dataset-public-pair); BQ). Terminal disclaimers are likewise absent from every current table (PAIR `DIST/DISQ/N574/P574` to 2016-03; PEDS to 2024-10; PatentsView `term_disclaimer` for ids 6,334,275-10,390,413, roughly to Aug 2019), so the front-page "Notice" field is a manual check or a documented limitation (BQ; DOM).

**Sources rejected for automation.** Google Patents has no API; `patents.google.com/robots.txt` allows `/patent/` but disallows everything else including the `/xhr/query` endpoint that `patent-client-agents` scrapes, and Google's Terms of Service (effective 2026-07-30) prohibit "using automated means to access content ... in violation of the machine-readable instructions" and list "scraping content that doesn't belong to you" as grounds for suspension; its "Legal status" is labelled "an assumption and is not a legal conclusion" ([Google Terms of Service](https://policies.google.com/terms); [robots.txt](https://patents.google.com/robots.txt); KLS; GH). PatentsView's API and search are **paused** since the 2026-03-20 migration to ODP, the legacy API has returned 410 since 2025-05-01, the old S3 bucket returns 403, and the new PatentSearch API needs an `X-Api-Key` ([USPTO: PatentsView migrating to ODP](https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2026/patentsview-migrating-uspto-open-data-portal-march-20); KLS; GH). Lens exposes only a computed `legal_status` (`INACTIVE` = lapsed but revivable) behind an approval-gated, non-commercial API with no event list ([Lens API: patent response](https://docs.api.lens.org/response-patent.html); KLS). Espacenet and PATENTSCOPE web UIs prohibit robots (10 search actions/minute thresholds), and PATSTAT is paid ([PATENTSCOPE terms](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/patentscope/data/terms_patentscope); KLS). Ownership comes from USPTO Patent Assignment Search (`assignmentcenter.uspto.gov`, interactive) or the stale `uspto_oce_assignment` tables (record_dt to 2017-02-07) (README; BQ).

## Status is derived from dated event sequences, and a later revival always beats an earlier lapse

The agent's central rule set comes from three vocabularies that describe the same lifecycle. The USPTO MaintFeeEvents/PEDS/PAIR vocabulary, the INPADOC vocabulary served by OPS and displayed on Google Patents, and the OG notice lists must be mapped onto one internal event enum before any status logic runs. The map below is assembled from the recovered 2018 USPTO documentation, the EPO's weekly `EN-Legal-event-codes.xlsx` (4,688 rows, 77 for authority US), the BigQuery PAIR/PEDS code tables and the OG page format ([EN-Legal-event-codes.xlsx](https://link.epo.org/web/coverage/weekly/EN-Legal-event-codes.xlsx); KLS; BQ).

| Meaning | USPTO MaintFeeEvents file (ODP) | PAIR / PEDS (BigQuery) | INPADOC (OPS / Google Patents) | OG notice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fee paid (4th/8th/12th yr) | `M1551`/`M1552`/`M1553` large, `M2551-3` small, `M3551-3` micro; older `M170-M185`, `M273-M285`, `F170-F175` | PEDS has `M15xx/M25xx/M35xx`; PAIR has **none** | `FPAY` (ended 2017-08-19), `MAFP` (2017-10→) | n/a |
| Reminder mailed | `REM.` | PEDS `REM.`; PAIR none | `REMI` (→2017-08), `FEPP` with free text "Maintenance fee reminder mailed" | n/a |
| Late surcharge | `M1554`-`M1556` (+ small/micro variants) | n/a | `SULP` (→2017-08) | n/a |
| Expired for nonpayment | `EXP.` (period is part of the code) | `EXP.` event; PAIR status **250**; PEDS status "Patent Expired Due to NonPayment ... 37 CFR 1.362" | `LAPS` (−), `STCH` (−), `FP` (−, carries effective date = anniversary) | row under "PATENTS WHICH EXPIRED ON <date>" |
| Petition on fees | `PMFP` filed, `PMFG` granted, `PMFD` denied, `PMFS` dismissed; `M1557` unavoidable / `M1558` unintentional surcharge | `PMFP/PMFG/PMFD/PMFS`, `MPMFG` mailed | n/a | n/a |
| Reinstated | `EXPX` "Patent Reinstated After Maintenance Fee Payment Confirmed" | `REIN` event; PAIR status **153** | `PRDP` (+, live; last US gazette 2026-09-28), `REIN` (→2017-07-23), `STCK` (→2017-12), `DP` (1986-97), `RN` rescission (→1989) | row under "Patents Reinstated ...", with Granted Date |
| Defective payment | `F160`-`F169` (too early, misidentified, not negotiable, insufficient deposit account) | n/a | `FPIR` | n/a |
| Terminal disclaimer | n/a | `DIST`, `DISQ`, `N574`, `P574` | `DC` "DISCLAIMER FILED" | n/a |

Two negatives matter as much as the positives: **there is no `LAPSE`, `REIN` or `REINS` code in the USPTO MaintFeeEvents file** (expiry is `EXP.`, reinstatement is `EXPX`), and **`EXPY` is not a US INPADOC code** ([MaintFeeEvents documentation](https://web.archive.org/web/20241210103618id_/https://bulkdata.uspto.gov/data/patent/maintenancefee/MaintFeeEventsFileDocumentation.doc); KLS). The INPADOC feed was re-coded in 2017: `FPAY`/`REMI`/`SULP`/`REIN` stop in Aug 2017 and `MAFP`/`FEPP`/`PRDP` take over, so a parser must treat {`FPAY`, `MAFP`} as payment, {`REMI`, `FEPP`+"reminder"} as reminder, {`LAPS`, `FP`, `STCH`} as lapse and {`PRDP`, `REIN`, `STCK`} as revival ([INPADOC coverage XLSX](https://link.epo.org/web/coverage/weekly/EN-Contents-coverage-INPADOC-legal-event-data.xlsx); KLS). Three independent 2026 codebases (`matusfaro/invented`, `pipeworx-io/mcp-patent-maintenance-fees`, `hollomancer/sbir-analytics`) converged on the same rule for the USPTO file: lapsed = an `EXP.` event with no **later** `EXPX` ([ingest-expirations.ts](https://github.com/matusfaro/invented/blob/main/pipeline/ingest-expirations.ts); GH).

Dates carry different meanings and the agent must not conflate them. The legal expiry date is always the **end of the 4th, 8th or 12th anniversary of grant**; a weekend or holiday extends the payment day, not the expiry date (README rules from MPEP 2500 / 37 CFR 1.362). Event entry dates are processing dates: for 8,646,606 the INPADOC `FP` carries effective date 2026-02-11 (the 12th anniversary) while `LAPS`/`STCH` are entered 2026-03-16 and `FP` on 2026-04-07, five to eight weeks later; the 2018 USPTO sample shows `REM.` 1985-08-12 then `EXP.` 1985-10-31 for a 1985-09-29 anniversary ([Google Patents US8646606B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8646606B2/en); KLS). Use `FP`'s effective date, or compute the anniversary, for "expired on <date>" (KLS). INPADOC lapse data is current to within about two weeks of the weekly file, while reinstatements lag by months (KLS).

The proposed rules, which the notes derive from the statute, the code semantics and the worked example, are these. **Expired for nonpayment on D**: D = grant + 4/8/12 years for the first unpaid stage; evidence tiers are (1) an OG row under "PATENTS WHICH EXPIRED ON D", (2) INPADOC `FP` with effective date D or `LAPS`/`STCH` entered 4-8 weeks after D, (3) USPTO file `EXP.` on or after D; and no later revival event may exist in any source (KLS). **Reinstated on R**: R is the OG Granted Date, or the `PMFG`/`EXPX` entry date, or the `PRDP` gazette date (coarsest); status becomes "in force (reinstated); intervening rights possible for D..R" (KLS; README). **In grace period**: today lies between the fee due date (anniversary minus six months) and the anniversary, with no payment event for that stage and possibly a reminder; no `EXP.`/`LAPS`/OG row can exist yet, so the label is "fee unpaid, not yet expired; expires D unless paid" (KLS). **Pending/ambiguous**: a lapse event plus a later payment or petition code (`M1553`, `M1558`, `PMFP`) without `EXPX`/`PMFG`/`PRDP`/OG reinstatement row means "petition pending or payment not yet confirmed" and is flagged conflicting (KLS). **Conflicting evidence**: when the OG says reinstated but INPADOC/Google still say lapsed (8,646,606 today), trust the later-dated official notice and record both; when the OG says expired but INPADOC shows no `LAPS`, that is INPADOC lag; a payment code dated after D without a surcharge or petition code is a likely short or misapplied payment (`F16x`) and is flagged; duplicate `EXP.` rows (patent 4294839 has two identical rows in the sample data) are deduplicated on (patent, date, code); an `RN` or `PMFD` after a lapse means the notice was rescinded or the petition denied and forces re-evaluation (KLS). **Expired at end of term** is never signalled by `FP`/`EXP.` or an OG nonpayment row; it is computed from 20 years after the earliest US non-provisional filing plus PTA/PTE, or a terminal disclaimer, and the notes warn that the ODP file "doesn't carry PTA, so filing+20y would mislabel live patents as dead" ([ingest-expirations.ts](https://github.com/matusfaro/invented/blob/main/pipeline/ingest-expirations.ts); GH; BQ). A PEDS gotcha closes the list: the status category can stay "Patent Expired..." after a `REIN` event (sample sequence `EXP.` 2007-06-06 → `REIN` 2007-06-07 → `EXP.` 2007-06-07), so always read the event list, not the status string (BQ).

The README's domain rules bound all of this: maintenance fees apply only to **utility and reissue utility** patents (design and plant never "expire for nonpayment"), fee windows are 3-3.5 / 7-7.5 / 11-11.5 years with surcharge grace to year 4 / 8 / 12, reinstatement petitions filed after **2026-08-13** need additional information and the higher fee once more than **1 year** has passed (previously 2 years), each reissue in a family has needed its own fee since 2018-01-16, a terminal disclaimer caps term at the related patent's expiry and is checked in the front-page "Notice" field, and expiry of one patent gives no right to practise if other unexpired patents cover the product (README, citing references 01-14). Identifier normalisation feeds these rules: the canonical key is `(country="US", number without leading zeros, optional kind)` serialised DOCDB-style as `US-7653958-B2` to match BigQuery and OPS, matching on `(country, number)` because OG rows and MaintFeeEvents omit the kind; `RE`, `D`, `PP`, `H`, `T` prefixes are preserved and design/plant/SIR are rejected by the status stage with a reason; application numbers are stored as 8-digit compact `13031042` with display `13/031,042`, and "application vs patent" is never inferred from magnitude because patent numbers passed 12,000,000 in 2024 and collide with 8-digit serials ([uspto_pfw_mcp identifier_resolution.py](https://github.com/john-walkoe/uspto_pfw_mcp/blob/main/src/patent_filewrapper_mcp/util/identifier_resolution.py); GH). No PyPI package normalises patent identifiers; GH supplies a 20-row test table (`7,653,958` → `US-7653958`; `RE.46,721` → `US-RE46721-E`; `D812345` → flagged `not_subject_to_maintenance_fees`; `13/031,042` → `13031042`) for the in-repo module (GH).

## A deterministic LangGraph workflow with two interrupt gates, not an autonomous agent

**Version set.** The compatible pair today is **LangGraph 1.2.20** (PyPI 2026-11-05) and **LangChain 1.4.9** (PyPI 2026-11-04; its pyproject pins `langgraph>=1.2.12,<1.3.0`), with langchain-core 1.7.6, langgraph-checkpoint 4.3.1, langgraph-checkpoint-sqlite 3.1.3, langgraph-prebuilt 1.1.1, langchain-litellm 0.11.2 and litellm 1.108.2; langchain-litellm's pyproject excludes litellm 1.107.0 with the comment "1.107.0 is malware (litellm incident, 2026-10-21)" ([PyPI langgraph](https://pypi.org/project/langgraph/); [langchain-litellm pyproject](https://github.com/Akshay-Dongare/langchain-litellm/blob/4dfac1e/pyproject.toml); LG). The project's `llm/` package already pins langchain-core 1.7.6, langchain-litellm 0.11.2 and litellm 1.108.2; `FreeLLM.chat_model()` returns `ChatLiteLLMRouter(router=..., model=group, model_name=group)`, every deployment is named `openai/<model_id>` with an `api_base`, and `bind_tools`, `with_structured_output` (default `json_schema`; `method="function_calling"` for weaker models), `stream`, `astream` and `ainvoke` were verified live; the `agent/` directory is empty and `example/` shows the `[tool.uv.sources] freellm = { path = "../llm", editable = true }` pattern to reuse (LG, citing `llm/src/freellm/router.py` and `docs/llm/routing-and-ranking.md`). A new `agent/` project therefore adds `langgraph>=1.2.20,<1.3`, `langchain>=1.4.9,<1.5` and `langgraph-checkpoint-sqlite>=3.1.3,<4` (LG).

**Workflow, not agent.** Anthropic defines workflows as "systems where LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths" and agents as systems that "dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage", recommends "the simplest solution possible", and says workflows "offer predictability and consistency for well-defined tasks" ([Anthropic, Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents); METH; LG). The brief fixes the eleven stages in advance, says a single agent is fine, and says multiple agents need an engineering justification; Anthropic's own numbers are that justification against multi-agent, since agents use about 4× the tokens of chat and multi-agent systems about 15×, and same-model agents "fail in correlated ways" so a second LLM voice is not an independent check ([Anthropic, multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [Anthropic, multiagent systems](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems); METH). The recommendation across LG and METH is therefore: deterministic code owns control flow and every data-source call; the LLM is confined to five bounded calls, each with a small Pydantic schema: (1) plan drafting from the request, (2) optional query expansion (CPC/keyword synonyms), (3) per-candidate technical summary plus powered/non-powered classification with mandatory `evidence_ids` and verbatim `quotes`, (4) opportunity rationale text, (5) report prose paragraphs (LG). If a genuine tool loop is wanted for the demo, it is confined to one optional node (for example "fetch extra claim text") using `ToolNode` with `handle_tool_errors` and a call cap, gated on the probed capability of the selected model (LG). A survey of six open-source deep-research agents (open_deep_research, gpt-researcher, STORM, AgentLaboratory, deer-flow, deepagents) found that only ODR's *legacy* graph uses `interrupt()` for plan approval; the others end the turn or block on `input()`, and the 2026 designs (deepagents, DeerFlow 2.x) converge on `create_agent` + middleware, "a pattern that suits open-ended chat agents more than this fixed, auditable pipeline" ([open_deep_research @1b7d2e8](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research/tree/1b7d2e8); LG).

**Stage-to-primitive mapping.** LG verified the following names against the 1.2.20 source and proposes this mapping, which doubles as the architecture diagram the brief asks for:

| Stage | Primitive | Deterministic or LLM |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | plain node writing a Pydantic-validated `request` | code (CLI args) |
| Plan | `llm.with_structured_output(Plan, ...)` + `.with_retry()`, template fallback | LLM call 1 |
| Approval | `decision = interrupt(plan_payload, response_schema=ApprovalDecision)`; node returns `Command(goto="discover")` or `Command(goto="plan", update={"plan_feedback": [...]})` | human gate |
| Discovery | one node per adapter (`og_notices`, `bigquery`, `ops_status`, `odp_fixture`), each `add_node(..., retry_policy=RetryPolicy(max_attempts=4))`, emitting `get_stream_writer()` events; adapter failure records a `SourceOutage` evidence row instead of raising | code (+ LLM call 2 optional) |
| Normalisation | pure-code node (identifier/date canonicalisation) | code |
| Status verification | `Send("verify_candidate", {...})` fan-out into a candidate subgraph running the rules above; evidence timeline = list of `EvidenceRecord` | code |
| Technical analysis | LLM node inside the subgraph; code asserts every quoted snippet exists in fetched text | LLM call 3 |
| Opportunity screening | code scoring with weights visible in state; optional rationale | code (+ LLM call 4) |
| Human review | `interrupt(candidate_table, response_schema=ReviewDecision)`; resume dict maps candidate → keep/drop/flag | human gate |
| Generation | code node rendering docx/pptx from one `ValidatedDataset` | code (+ LLM call 5 for prose) |
| Validation | code node: schema, docx↔pptx equality, citation existence; routes back to Generation on failure, bounded | code |

The `interrupt()` semantics that shape the node code are documented and source-verified: "The graph resumes from the start of the node, re-executing all logic", so side effects before `interrupt()` must be idempotent or moved after it; "To use an `interrupt`, you must enable a checkpointer"; resume must reuse the same `thread_id` ("A new ID starts an empty thread"); never wrap `interrupt()` in a bare `try/except` or a `while True`, route back via `Command(goto=...)` instead; `response_schema` (requires `langgraph>=1.2.12`) validates the resume value and raises `pydantic.ValidationError` with nothing saved; `interrupt_before`/`interrupt_after` are "for debugging, not human-in-the-loop use" ([LangGraph interrupts docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/interrupts); [types.py L913-1010](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langgraph/blob/e5e37b5/libs/langgraph/langgraph/types.py); LG). Persistence is `SqliteSaver.from_conn_string("outputs/checkpoints.sqlite")` from `langgraph-checkpoint-sqlite` (3.1.3, which recommends `LANGGRAPH_STRICT_MSGPACK=true`); `InMemorySaver` is for tests only; after a process restart, `graph.get_state(config)` exposes `snapshot.interrupts` to re-render the pending question and `graph.invoke(Command(resume=answer), config)` continues ([Persistence docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/persistence); LG). Use `durability="sync"` during the demo so a hard kill leaves a consistent checkpoint; `"exit"` is unsuitable for a human-gated run (LG). `RetryPolicy` defaults are `max_attempts=3`, exponential backoff from 0.5 s, and `default_retry_on` retries `ConnectionError` and 5xx `httpx.HTTPStatusError`/`requests.HTTPError` but not `ValueError`-class errors ([types.py L430-470](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langgraph/blob/e5e37b5/libs/langgraph/langgraph/types.py); LG). Progress streams through `graph.stream(inputs, cfg, stream_mode=["updates","custom"])`, with interrupts appearing as `__interrupt__` in `updates` (v1) or in the `interrupts` field (v2); `stream_mode="tasks"` and `"checkpoints"` need a checkpointer and give task start/finish events; `get_state_history()` returns `StateSnapshot`s newest-first for audit and replay ([Streaming docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/streaming); [Time travel docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/use-time-travel); LG). For the per-candidate subgraph, the default `compile(checkpointer=None)` inherits the parent checkpointer, so interrupts and durability still work inside it ([Subgraphs docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/use-subgraphs); LG). `thread_id` convention: `run-<YYYYMMDD>-<slug>-<uuid7_short>`, one per end-to-end run, kept in a run manifest (LG). Patterns worth copying from the surveyed repos: ODR's `override_reducer` for resettable lists on re-plan, deer-flow's keyed-merge reducers for the evidence dict, deepagents' `Command(resume={"decisions": [...]})` payload with the `approve|edit|reject|respond` vocabulary from `HumanInTheLoopMiddleware`, gpt-researcher's exact-match approval sentinel and hard revision caps, and STORM's artifact-per-stage files with `run_config.json` and `llm_call_history.jsonl` ([human_in_the_loop.py](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langchain/blob/d9caf08/libs/langchain_v1/langchain/agents/middleware/human_in_the_loop.py); LG). Anti-patterns to avoid: blocking `input()` inside a node with no checkpointer, clarification-as-tool ending the run with `Command(goto=END)`, prompt-only citation numbering, catch-all exception swallowing, and 40-middleware stacks (LG).

**Evidence ledger.** No surveyed repo has a true evidence ledger; ODR numbers sources per tool call with prompt-only citations and STORM/gpt-researcher keep URL-keyed tables without claim-vs-snippet checks (LG). The proposal is a first-class state key `evidence: Annotated[dict[str, EvidenceRecord], merge_ledger]` so `Send` branches merge without clobbering, with `EvidenceRecord` fields `evidence_id` (deterministic `sha256(source_adapter|url|retrieved_at|snippet)[:12]`), `source_adapter` (`og_notice|bigquery|ops|odp|fixture`), `url`, `retrieved_at` (UTC ISO), `http_status`, `raw_snippet`, `content_sha256`, `parser_version`, `patent_number`, `event_type` (`expired_nonpayment|reinstated|grant|fee_paid|outage`), `event_date`, `is_synthetic`, exported to `schemas/` as JSON Schema (LG). Every LLM-facing schema carries `evidence_ids: list[str]` and, for quotes, `quote: str`; a code validator rejects outputs whose ids are not in the ledger or whose quote is not a substring of the referenced `raw_snippet` (LG). METH adds PROV-lite and grading fields to the same record (`source_grade` A-F, `credibility` 1-6, `derived_from` for computed dates), covered in the methodology section below. The ready-made verifier `citesure` 0.2.2 is not worth a dependency: its own ablation shows the default install reaches 38.0% agreement versus 86.1% with the NLI extra ([PyPI citesure](https://pypi.org/pypi/citesure/json); LG). deer-flow's `ToolReceiptMiddleware` receipt shape `{tool_call_id, tool_name, status, args_sha256, output_sha256, output_bytes, created_at}` is the model for the tool-call log ([deer-flow @b8e1ca9](https://github.com/bytedance/deer-flow/tree/b8e1ca9); LG).

**HTTP adapter layer.** One `httpx.AsyncClient` per source built by a factory that accepts a `transport`, so tests inject `httpx.MockTransport` or `respx`; `httpx` 0.28.1 retries only connect errors (`HTTPTransport(retries=n)`), so status-aware retries use `tenacity` 9.2.1 with `stop_after_attempt(4)`, `wait_exponential_jitter`, retry on transport errors and {429, 500, 502, 503, 504}, honour `Retry-After`, and **never** retry OPS quota 403s or other 4xx ([httpx transports docs](https://www.python-httpx.org/advanced/transports/); GH). Caching is `hishel`: 2.0.0 (released 2026-11-05) dropped RFC 9111 by default in favour of "simple mode" that keeps every successful GET until storage expiry, with `extensions={"caching": {"ttl": ...}}` per request, which GH judges a better fit than 1.x heuristics for OG pages that set no useful cache headers, provided the version is recorded in the data-source docs ([hishel migration.md](https://github.com/karpetrosyan/hishel/blob/master/docs/migration.md); GH). Rate limits are one `aiolimiter.AsyncLimiter` per source (OG about 1 req/s; OPS 1 req/s per the fair-use FAQ) (GH). Every evidence record is stamped `retrieved_at` and `from_cache`, following `patent-client-agents`' provenance hook and its rule that registered-IP data is proxied live with short TTLs because "the upstream register is always the system of record" ([CONNECTOR_STANDARDS.md](https://github.com/parkerhancock/patent-client-agents/blob/main/CONNECTOR_STANDARDS.md); GH). The source-outage demo is a `--simulate-outage <source>` flag that swaps in `httpx.MockTransport(lambda r: httpx.Response(503))` for one adapter; the pipeline marks affected evidence `unavailable` with a timestamp, continues with other sources, and the report shows the degraded-evidence label (GH; LG).

**Terminal UI.** For an 8-12 h `uv run` CLI whose pauses are LangGraph interrupts, LG recommends **Rich 15.0.0** (`Live` for the stage/progress table from `custom` events, `Table` for the plan and candidate/evidence views, `Prompt.ask(..., choices=[...])`/`Confirm.ask` for decisions) behind a **Typer 0.28.0** entry point, with `prompt_toolkit` 3.0.53 optional for multi-line "revise the plan" text; **Textual 8.2.8** is right only if a persistent full-screen dashboard is required, at the cost of `@work` workers and async tests ([Rich Live docs](https://rich.readthedocs.io/en/stable/live.html); [Textual Workers guide](https://textual.textualize.io/guide/workers/); LG). Rich fits the interrupt model exactly: stream inside `Live(transient=True)`, exit the block on `__interrupt__`, render, ask, re-enter and `stream(Command(resume=...))`; no threads (LG). Testability comes from injecting `ask_human: Callable[[InterruptPayload], Decision]` and a `Console(file=io.StringIO(), force_terminal=False)`, plus a `--non-interactive` mode that feeds scripted decisions for tests and the demo (LG). LangChain's own `deepagents-code` TUI is Textual + prompt-toolkit + Rich, about 52k lines, with an `ApprovalMenu` bound to `y/a/n/e/Tab`, which LG cites as precedent and as out of proportion for this build ([deepagents @6f9e494 libs/code](https://github.com/langchain-ai/deepagents/tree/6f9e494/libs/code); LG).

**Testing.** Drive graphs with `GenericFakeChatModel` from `langchain_core.language_models.fake_chat_models` (subclass with a no-op `bind_tools`; script `AIMessage`s including `tool_calls`; function-scoped fixtures because "an exhausted iterator cannot be reused"), compile a fresh graph with `InMemorySaver` per test, test single nodes via `graph.nodes["x"].invoke(...)`, run partial sections with `update_state(as_node=...)` + `interrupt_after`, and test interrupt/resume by streaming until `__interrupt__` then invoking `Command(resume=...)` ([LangChain unit-testing docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langchain/test/unit-testing); [LangGraph test docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/test); LG). HTTP fixtures are `pytest-recording` 0.14.1 / `vcrpy` 9.4.2 cassettes (`--record-mode=none` and `--block-network` in CI, `filter_headers=["authorization"]`), plus one hand-edited 503 cassette and `respx` 0.23.1 `side_effect=httpx.ConnectError` for outage tests; keep synthetic fixtures in `fixtures/` separate from cassettes as the brief requires ([PyPI pytest-recording](https://pypi.org/project/pytest-recording/); GH; LG). The layered plan: pure unit tests for Normalisation and the status rules over the six fixtures; adapter tests on cassettes; graph tests with scripted Plan/analysis JSON asserting the stream stops at `__interrupt__` then resuming with `approve` and with `revise ...`; a subprocess test that reloads the SQLite checkpointer and resumes in a new process; generation tests that reopen the docx/pptx and compare IDs (LG). The LiteLLM Router is tested offline with `mock_response` in `litellm_params` (a string, or `"litellm.RateLimitError"`), and tests pin `FREELLM_HOME` to a temp dir because late background callbacks once overwrote the real `stats.json` (LG, citing `docs/llm/routing-and-ranking.md`).

**Logging and cost.** The required "sanitised execution logs" are produced locally: one `outputs/runs/<thread_id>/events.jsonl` written by a sink that consumes the same `["updates","custom","tasks"]` stream the TUI renders, each line `{ts, thread_id, stage, node, event, duration_ms, adapter, http_status, bytes, evidence_ids, model, tokens_in, tokens_out, cost_usd}`; `structlog` 26.1.0 processors redact keys matching `(api_key|token|authorization|secret)` and truncate `raw_snippet` to 200 characters; a committed `docs/sample-logs/` copy is the deliverable ([structlog processors docs](https://www.structlog.org/en/stable/processors.html); LG). Token and cost accounting uses `UsageMetadataCallbackHandler` / `get_usage_metadata_callback()` from `langchain_core.callbacks.usage` plus `response_metadata["response_cost"]`, which `ChatLiteLLM` fills from LiteLLM's `_hidden_params.response_cost`; print a Rich table and write `cost.json`, stating that $0 is a property of the free deployments, not the model ([usage.py](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langchain/blob/d9caf08/libs/core/langchain_core/callbacks/usage.py); LG). LangSmith's Developer plan ($0, 1 seat, 5k traces/month, 14-day retention, env-var-only setup) is optional and documented as such in `.env.example` ([LangChain pricing](https://www.langchain.com/pricing); LG).

## Free models support tools and JSON schema unevenly, so probe per deployment and keep schemas small

The capability picture for the models the user can reach for free is a patchwork of provider self-reports, not measured success rates. LiteLLM's `model_prices_and_context_window.json` (4,768 entries, fetched 2026-11-07) marks Groq's `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, `openai/gpt-oss-20b` and `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` as `supports_function_calling=True, supports_response_schema=True`, the Gemini flash family (`gemini-2.5-flash` through `gemini-3.8-flash`, `gemini-flash-latest`) as tools + json_schema, Cerebras `gpt-oss-120b`/`qwen-3.8-27b`/`gemma-4-31b` as both, but **OVH `gpt-oss-120b`/`gpt-oss-20b` as `supports_function_calling=False`** (json_schema True) while OVH `Qwen3-32B`, `Mistral-Small-3.2-24B` and the Llama 3 models support both ([LiteLLM model map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BerriAI/litellm/main/model_prices_and_context_window.json); LG). Groq itself says strict schema enforcement (constrained decoding) exists only on four models (`gpt-oss-20b`, `gpt-oss-120b`, `qwen3.8-27b`, `muse-spark-open`), best-effort mode "can return HTTP 400 errors", every other model is JSON Object Mode only, strict mode requires every property in `required` and `additionalProperties:false`, and "Streaming and tool use are not currently supported with Structured Outputs" ([Groq structured outputs docs](https://console.groq.com/docs/structured-outputs); LG). OpenRouter's keyless `/api/v1/models` lists **16 text models priced 0/0**; nearly all advertise `tools` but only 8 advertise `structured_outputs`, support "is determined per endpoint, not just per model", and `provider: {"require_parameters": true}` routes only to endpoints that honour `response_format` ([OpenRouter structured outputs docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/structured-outputs); LG).

Rate limits bound the run budget more than the quality does. Groq's Developer-plan base limits for the three structured-output models are **30 RPM, 1K RPD, 8K TPM, 200K TPD** with `x-ratelimit-*` headers and `retry-after` only on 429 ([Groq rate limits docs](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits); LG). The project's own verified figures (2026-11-07) are OpenRouter `:free` 20 RPM / 50 RPD (1,000 RPD after a $10 top-up), Groq ~30 RPM, Gemini 5-30 RPM and 20-1,500 RPD per model (docs figure, not re-verified), OVH 2 RPM anonymous, LLM7 anonymous rate-limits quickly, and 33 models worked with the user's keys; reasoning models (GLM-5.3-Flash, gpt-oss) put output in `reasoning_content` so `content` can be empty with small `max_tokens`, hence **`max_tokens >= 512`** (LG, citing `docs/llm/free-llm-providers.md`). Because freellm names every deployment `openai/<model_id>` with an `api_base`, LiteLLM's provider-specific capability tables and translation layers are bypassed: `response_format` and `tools` pass through as OpenAI-compatible JSON and the real provider decides, so capability must be probed per deployment at runtime (extend freellm's `make check` chat probe with a tiny `response_format` and `tools` probe and cache the result in `stats.json`) (LG).

On the LangChain side, `ChatLiteLLM.with_structured_output(schema, *, method="json_schema"|"function_calling"|"json_mode", include_raw=False, strict=None)` is source-verified: `json_schema` sets `strict=True` by default, converts via `convert_to_openai_tool`, applies `_ensure_additional_properties_false` recursively and binds `response_format={"type":"json_schema",...}`; `function_calling` forces `tool_choice="required"` unless the model refuses forced tools, in which case it warns and binds without a forced choice, raising `OutputParserException` when no tool call returns; `json_mode` binds `{"type":"json_object"}` and parses with `PydanticOutputParser`; `include_raw=True` returns `{"raw","parsed","parsing_error"}` ([langchain_litellm/chat_models/litellm.py](https://github.com/Akshay-Dongare/langchain-litellm/blob/4dfac1e/langchain_litellm/chat_models/litellm.py); LG). Two open issues argue for `invoke`/`ainvoke` rather than `stream` on any call that returns tool calls or structured output: #392 "Invalid tool calls are dropped from the assistant message" and #379 "ChatLiteLLMRouter keeps one raw tool_calls entry per streamed chunk" ([langchain-litellm issues](https://github.com/Akshay-Dongare/langchain-litellm/issues); LG). LangChain 1.4.9's `create_agent(response_format=ToolStrategy|ProviderStrategy)` exists, but for this fixed pipeline LG prefers `with_structured_output(..., include_raw=True)` in plain nodes so each node can log raw text and run its own repair and retry without raising ([Structured output docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langchain/structured-output); LG).

The mitigation stack, in order: (a) `method="json_schema"` with `include_raw=True` on providers that advertise it, else `json_mode` + `PydanticOutputParser` + a code-side repair pass (strip fences, `json_repair`-style brace trimming) + one re-ask that includes the validation error; (b) schemas of at most 8 flat fields, enums over free text, no `Union`; (c) one LLM call per node, never multi-tool loops; (d) a deterministic fallback for every LLM node (template plan; "classification: unknown → needs human review") so a model outage degrades rather than aborts the run; (e) `max_tokens >= 512`; (f) route structured/tool-heavy nodes to the `free-tools` model group and prose nodes to `free` (LG). gpt-researcher's `parse_json_markdown(response, parser=json_repair.loads)` with a shape normaliser and fallback-to-input, and ODR's `.with_retry(stop_after_attempt=...)` on every structured call, are the copyable precedents ([gpt-researcher @3582a68](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/tree/3582a68); LG). No systematic third-party benchmark of tool-calling reliability for free models was found (LG).

## Both deliverables render from one validated Pydantic model and are re-opened to prove it

The supporting set, used only together with a core hit or a household indexing code, is A46B 1-11, 15, 17 with indexing codes A46B 2200/302 (broom), /3026 (dusting brush), /3033 (household brush), /304 (lavatory brush), /3053, /306; B08B 1/* (cleaning with tools: 1/12 brushes, 1/14 wipes/sponges, 1/16 blades/scrapers, 1/40 integrated fluid dispensing); and B25G 1/*, 3/* (handles) ([USPTO CPC scheme A46B](https://www.uspto.gov/web/patents/classification/cpc/html/cpc-A46B.html); DOM). D06F (laundry), A47L 15/00 (dishwashers) and A47J 43/28 (culinary implements) are out of scope (DOM). `cpc.definition` (2026-06-01) on BigQuery supplies the titles; A47L 13/00 mixes powered and non-powered devices, so a non-powered filter must be applied to claims text (BQ).

**Powered-device exclusion.** The scheme itself labels the powered groups, so co-classification is the primary signal: reject if any CPC is in A47L 1/02, A47L 5/00-9/00 (suction cleaners), A47L 11/* except 11/32-11/33, A47L 21/02, A47L 23/02, 23/06, 23/263, A46B 13/02-13/06 (keep 13/08 hand-driven), A47K 7/04, B08B 3/* (liquid/steam), B08B 5/* (air flow), A61L, H02, H01M, H05B, F22B (DOM). The secondary pass is a keyword rule over title, abstract and the first independent claim: reject on `motor|motorized|electric|electrical|battery|batteries|rechargeable|charger|power supply|vacuum|suction|steam|heater|heating element|ultrasonic|ultraviolet|UV|LED|sensor|controller|pump` *when the term is a required element of an independent claim*; occurrences only in the description or dependent claims are a flag for human review, not a rejection (DOM, flagged as a design proposal whose thresholds are not from literature). Hand-pumped dispensers (A47L 13/22, 13/26, B08B 1/40), trigger sprayers and spring-loaded wringers are manual and stay; electric pumps, aerosol or compressed-gas propulsion and any heater are excluded (DOM). USPC Class 15 "BRUSHING, SCRUBBING, AND GENERAL CLEANING" (e.g. 15/228 mops and heads, 15/245 squeegee, 15/300.1 with air blast or suction) is a secondary filter only for grants before about 2015, because the USPTO stopped assigning USPC to utility grants in 2015 ([USPTO classification](https://www.uspto.gov/classification); DOM).

**Volume and base rates.** Counted on BigQuery `patentsview` (US utility grants, 1.21 GB scan): the core CPC union yields roughly **115-300 US utility grants per year** for 2000-2021 (median ~165; 2019: 257; 2020: 300), of which **75-85% carry no powered co-classification** (2020: 202 of 300; 2014: 175 of 213); A47L 13 alone is 75-180 grants per year (100-140 after removing powered co-classes); data end at 2021 because the snapshot is dated 2022-02 ([patents-public-data.patentsview](https://console.cloud.google.com/marketplace/product/google_patents_public_datasets/google-patents-public-data); DOM). On renewal, Patently-O (Crouch, 2026-03-15) reports from USPTO maintenance-fee records that "roughly 60% of all patentees now abandon their patents before the full term expires" and the full-term maintenance rate "has fallen to approximately 40%" (peak ~52% in 2012-13; the 11.5-year large-entity fee rose from $3,100 in 2001 to $8,280 in January 2025); Moore (2005) found 53.7% of the 1991 cohort lapsed for nonpayment; the stage-wise 86%/67%/44% figures circulating online are secondhand and unverified ([Patently-O](https://patentlyo.com/patent/2026/03/are-rising-maintenance-fees-shortening-the-effective-patent-term.html); [Moore, GMU WP 04-29](https://law.gmu.edu/pubs/papers/04_29); DOM). "Expired for nonpayment" is therefore the modal fate of a US patent, which cuts against treating lapse as a rarity signal of hidden value (DOM).

**Related-patent risk signals.** Six relationship types are detectable from public bibliographic data, each a reason the candidate's lapse may not free the product: (1) same simple family (`family_id`, `priority_claim`); (2) continuation/divisional/CIP lineage (`parent`/`child` `type`; stale `uspto_oce_pair.continuity_*`; front-page "Related U.S. Application Data"); (3) terminal disclaimers (front-page notice only; not in `patents.publications`); (4) forward citations (`cited_by`, 24 GB); (5) same-assignee later grants in the same CPC group (`assignee_harmonized`); (6) reissues (RE numbers, maintained separately since 2018-01-16) (DOM; BQ). The TD-linked sibling is the strongest flag because MPEP 804 nonstatutory double patenting means the Office found the claims not patentably distinct and 37 CFR 1.321(c) makes the TD'd patent enforceable only while commonly owned; a live continuation may claim the same embodiment from another angle; a CIP "adds new matter, often the commercial improvement" and is the most likely blocker for a modernised version; forward citations in the same CPC main group with later filing dates are candidate blockers for improvements ([MPEP 804](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s804.html); [MPEP 1490](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s1490.html); DOM). The agent should emit each related record with relationship type, source field, evidence string and a "status to verify" flag, and never assert the related record's status without its own evidence timeline (DOM). A TD filed after the issue fee needs a certificate of correction to appear on the front page, and a reissue's front page "will not indicate that the patent is subject to a terminal disclaimer", so absence of a front-page notice is not proof of absence (DOM).

**Opportunity indicators and the honest framing of non-renewal.** The literature's bibliographic value signals are all computable from BigQuery fields: forward citations (Hall-Jaffe-Trajtenberg 2005: an extra citation per patent boosts market value by 3%), family size (Harhoff et al. 2003), claim count and first-independent-claim word count (Kuhn-Thompson 2019: longer = narrower, "ninety-five percent of first claims have less than 375 words"), CPC group count (Lerner-style scope, weak per Kuhn-Thompson), renewal history (Pakes 1986; Schankerman-Pakes 1986), and owner type (Bessen 2008: small patentees' patents "much less valuable"; `entity_status` is the free proxy) ([Hall, Jaffe & Trajtenberg](https://ideas.repec.org/a/rje/randje/v36y20051p16-38.html); [Kuhn & Thompson](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ijecbs/v26y2019i1p5-38.html); [Bessen 2008](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:respol:v:37:y:2008:i:5:p:932-945); DOM). The OECD (Squicciarini, Dernis, Criscuolo 2013) packaged these as cohort-normalised indicators with SQL, but its full indicator list could not be read (403/404 to automated fetches) ([IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/p/oec/stiaaa/2013-3-en.html); DOM). The honest framing, which the brief's "not free technology" instruction demands: under the standard renewal models non-renewal reveals that the owner judged the expected value below the fee (a small-entity first fee is a few hundred dollars), so lapse is prima facie evidence of **low private value to that owner**; the opportunity thesis must rest on reasons that valuation does not bind a new entrant (owner exited the business; individual inventor without manufacturing; owner has a newer generation under live patents; category economics changed) (DOM). Score "commercial relevance" on product-category evidence, "engineering effort" on the mechanism, keep "evidence quality" as a separate axis, and never let a bibliometric score masquerade as market demand; a 12-year lapse (two fees paid) signals more sustained private value than a 4-year lapse; a highly cited lapsed patent usually has patented successors, which raises related-patent risk even as it raises interest (DOM). The caveat on claim breadth is specific to expired patents: short independent claims mean more of the design space is now public, but also that live patents are more likely to have designed around it (DOM).

**Implementation challenges and unresolved risks.** For a mop/brush/squeegee-class product the implementation section covers materials and process (injection moulding, extrusion, bristle insertion, assembly), tooling cost and lead time (vendor figures with wide ranges: aluminium moulds ~$1,500-$16,000, production steel ~$5,000-$20,000, complex multi-cavity up to ~$100,000, flagged as marketing content), and the US regulatory floor: CPSC general-use duties (no GCC unless a specific rule applies, no mandatory third-party testing, 24-hour Section 15(b) reporting), FHSA labelling if a hazardous chemical is bundled, FIFRA device and treated-article rules if antimicrobial claims are made (EPA PR Notice 2000-1 names "sponges, mops" as treated articles; the exemption excludes public-health claims), FPLA labelling, Prop 65 for California (OEHHA text not retrieved; cite after human confirmation), and voluntary ASTM F2367 (brooms) / F2368 (wet mops) labelling specs ([CPSC GCC page](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/General-Certificate-of-Conformity); [40 CFR 152.25](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/152.25); DOM). The unresolved-risks checklist: each related record with its own status-to-verify; TD not checkable from BigQuery (state it); forward citations in the same main group filed after the candidate; same-assignee live grants; any RE number; design patents (15-year term, no maintenance fees, never "expire for nonpayment") covering the ornamental look; trade dress/trademarks; the reinstatement window (a patent lapsed less than 1 year is high reinstatement risk under the 2026-08-13 rule); intervening rights protect only activity started during the lapse; US-only scope (DOM; README).

**Market context.** The only free, primary, current US numbers found are the BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey "Housekeeping supplies" line, **$844 per consumer unit in 2025** and $877 in 2024 (release of 2026-10-29), mirrored on FRED as `CXUHKPGSUPPLB0101M` (2021 $803; 2022 $787; 2023 $818; 2024 $877) ([BLS CE news release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cesan.nr0.htm); [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CXUHKPGSUPPLB0101M); DOM). ## Method: spec, four ADRs, evals first, and evidence labels computed from rules, never from the model

**Anthropic guidance applied.** The repeated advice from Dec 2024 through 2026 is: start with the simplest workflow, invest in tool definitions ("as you would a docstring for a junior developer"), keep the generator and evaluator separate because "agents reliably skew positive when grading their own work", give the agent "something it can run that comes back pass or fail", and practise eval-driven development with 20-50 real tasks graded by code where possible ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents); [Anthropic, Harness design](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps); METH). "Show the plan before execution" so oversight shifts "from the individual step to the overall strategy" is the brief's Plan → Approval stage ([Anthropic, Trustworthy agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/trustworthy-agents); METH). Context engineering says to keep patent full text and OG pages out of the prompt and pass identifiers, fetching on demand, and to persist the evidence timeline as a structured file rather than conversation state ([Anthropic, Effective context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents); METH). The three ideas most worth copying are a separate citation/attribution pass (the CitationAgent), a sceptical evaluator that is not the generator, and deterministic pass/fail checks as primary verification with an LLM rubric only for prose (METH). "Loop engineering" and "graph engineering" are 2026 social-media labels, not methodologies (LangChain: "the latest name for a well established approach"); "harness engineering" has arXiv backing (2604.25850; 2609.00006) and its lesson is that tools, middleware and memory matter more than prompt prose ([LangChain blog](https://www.langchain.com/blog/3-years-of-graph-engineering-with-langgraph); [arXiv:2604.25850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25850); METH). Classify the project as a *static-workflow, single-agent, API-retrieval* deep-research agent in the architecture doc, following the taxonomy of arXiv 2506.18096 (METH).

**Process artefacts within 8-12 h.** Write `docs/SPEC.md` (about one page) as the brief's checklist in EARS form ("WHEN a candidate appears in an OG expiration notice AND a later OG reinstatement notice THE SYSTEM SHALL set status=reinstated and attach both notices") with explicit out-of-scope lines; those criteria double as regression-eval tasks ([Kiro docs: specs](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/); [GitHub Spec Kit blog](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/generative-ai/spec-driven-development-with-ai-get-started-with-a-new-open-source-toolkit/); METH). Write four MADR-minimal ADRs numbered after the repo's existing `adr-001`: **ADR-002** single-agent chained workflow (with the 15× multi-agent token figure as the rejected-option reason); **ADR-003** data-source adapter interface and the ODP adapter marked "requires ID.me-verified key"; **ADR-004** evidence and status model (dated claims, independent-source rule, reinstatement scan, fixed confidence vocabulary); **ADR-005** one validated JSON feeds both docx and pptx; each with a one-line "Confirmation" naming the test that checks it ([MADR](https://adr.github.io/madr/); [Nygard](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions); METH). Only these pass Nygard's "architecturally significant" test; library choices, prompt wording and chart styling go in the README or spec (METH). The README architecture section is the LangGraph graph drawn as the diagram with deterministic gates marked (METH). The report's methodology and limitations section follows WIPO 8.7.1 (search strategy, data preprocessing, analysis methods, issues and limitations), RIPL items 5/6/11/12/15/17 and PRISMA 2020 items 6/7/8/15/23b/23c/27, including a PRISMA-style flow count (candidates found → after non-powered filter → after status check → after reinstatement scan → recommended) and the exact queries in an appendix; WIPO's own distinction between a landscape report and an FTO/clearance opinion (§4.6.4) makes the brief's label a methodology statement rather than boilerplate ([WIPO Pub 946](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/pubdocs/en/wipo_pub_946.pdf); [RIPL statement](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:d410055d-605b-4d21-b26a-28ed0e98030c); [PRISMA 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8005924/); METH). Suggested time split: ~1 h spec + ADRs, ~2 h fixtures and eval harness, ~5-6 h adapters/graph/validation, ~1.5 h docx/pptx, ~1 h README, limitations and time/cost log (an estimate, METH).

**Evidence grading and uncertainty labelling.** Combine two per-source and per-claim schemes: Admiralty source reliability A-F and information credibility 1-6, "each assessed in isolation" (an OG notice is an A source; a single notice is credibility 2 "probably true" until later reinstatement lists have been scanned; a BigQuery row is A for bibliographic fields but 6 "cannot be judged" for status), with IPCC-style confidence derived from evidence (limited/medium/robust) × agreement (low/medium/high) into very low … very high, and a calibrated likelihood word only when a rule justifies it ("very likely expired for nonpayment" requires an OG expiry row plus a reinstatement scan covering every later published week; "about as likely as not" when sources conflict) ([Admiralty code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_code); [IPCC AR5 Box TS.1](https://sites.pitt.edu/~jdnorton/teaching/2682_confirmation/2017/slides/IPCC2014%20Uncertainty%20Fragment.pdf); METH). ICD 203's seven-term bands (almost no chance 1-5% … almost certain 95-99%) are the alternative vocabulary; pick one, print the mapping in the methodology section, and never let the LLM invent adverbs ([ODNI ICD 203](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf); METH). GRADE's separation of certainty of evidence from strength of recommendation maps directly onto keeping `status_confidence` and `opportunity_recommendation` as separate fields and sentences ([GRADE Handbook](https://gdt.gradepro.org/app/handbook/handbook.html); METH). Confidence labels are computed from evidence features (number of independent sources, recency, presence of a post-expiry reinstatement scan), never from the model's self-report, because LLMs "tend to be overconfident" and confident answers in one study had a 47% error rate ([Xiong et al. 2306.13063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063); [Zhou et al. 2401.06730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06730); METH). Each evidence item carries PROV-lite fields (`source_url`, `retrieved_at`, adapter/version as Activity, `derived_from` for computed dates) ([W3C PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/); METH). The legal citator is the right mental model: an OG expiry notice is a citation, and the "Shepardize" step is scanning every later "Patents Reinstated" list before calling a status current; two citators disagreeing is the brief's conflicting-evidence fixture ([Jenkins Law citator guide](https://guides.jenkinslaw.org/federal-caselaw/case-verification); METH). The structured-analytic transfers are a small Analysis of Competing Hypotheses over {expired-nonpayment, reinstated, in-grace, expired-end-of-term, never-expired} and two short lists in the report: Key Assumptions ("OG online window covers 52 weeks"; "BigQuery grant data current to X") and Quality of Information ([CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf); METH).

**Validation stage checklist (deterministic first).** (1) Schema validity of candidates and every evidence item; (2) citation recall: every status, date and ownership claim in prose maps to ≥1 evidence item (ALCE); (3) citation precision, FACT-style but exact-match: re-read each cited notice row or record and compare patent number, event type and date, flagging rather than silently fixing; (4) status-over-time check: the reinstatement scan covers all OG weeks published after the expiry notice, else downgrade confidence and say why; (5) README domain rules: utility/reissue only, anniversary expiry dates, 1-year vs 2-year threshold by petition date, TD note if applicable; (6) cross-document consistency of IDs, statuses, scores and recommendations across JSON, docx and pptx; (7) Key Assumptions and Quality of Information lists present; (8) the label present verbatim; (9) only then an LLM rubric (readability, completeness, instruction-following) by a model instance that did not draft the prose, with order randomised ([DeepResearch Bench FACT](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11763); [ALCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627); METH). The justification for exact-match over an LLM judge is quantitative: even the best commercial deep-research systems leave 10-22% of citations unsupported (Perplexity 90.24%, Gemini 81.44%, OpenAI 77.96% citation accuracy), automatic attribution judges top out near 80% macro-F1, and legal AI research tools "each hallucinate between 17% and 33% of the time" ([AttributionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15089); [Magesh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362); METH). Evals: the six fixtures are a balanced regression suite graded on the outcome (the status record and the docx/pptx fields), plus 10-20 capability tasks from real OG rows such as 8,646,606, plus negative cases the agent must not flag (design patent, plant patent, paid in grace); make the suite a pass/fail command listed in CLAUDE.md; re-run after every prompt edit because generic prompt additions can regress specific behaviours (Commey 2026: RAG citation compliance fell from 26/30 to 9/30) ([Commey 2601.22025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22025); METH).

## Conclusion

The research changes the problem's shape. "No USPTO API key" looked like a blocker for status evidence, but the keyless surface turns out to be the *official* one: the Official Gazette lists are the USPTO's legally operative publication of expirations and reinstatements, and the deep `sol/og` archive extends them eighteen years back, whereas the key-gated MaintFeeEvents file and the registered-free INPADOC feed are richer in event granularity but either inaccessible or lagging. The design consequence is that the agent's core competence is not search but **status-over-time verification**: an expiry row is a claim, and the later reinstatement lists are the citator that makes it current. None of the surveyed research-agent literature handles facts that later events reverse, while legal citator practice does; that is the defensible novelty to put at the centre of the 20-minute demo, with US 8,646,606 as the live case where the official notice and the two most-used secondary displays disagree.

The second shift is that the weak free models stop being a risk once the LLM's job is reduced to five schema-bound calls whose outputs cite ledger IDs that code verifies. Every quantitative result in the notes points the same way: provider-reported tool support is uneven, verbalised confidence is miscalibrated, LLM attribution judges plateau near 80% F1, and the OG/INPADOC/USPTO data are structured enough that exact-match re-checks beat any judge. The build should therefore spend its hours on adapters, the rules engine, the ledger and the round-trip validation of the two Office files, and should treat the prompts as the least load-bearing part of the system.

## Decisions the design must make

1. **Primary status source and URL pattern.** Options: (a) `www.uspto.gov/web/offices/com/sol/og/<YYYY>/week<NN>/TOC.htm` archive (2008→) with `patentsgazette.uspto.gov/week<NN>/OG/TOC.htm` as fallback; (b) 52-week site only. For (a): verified 200 pages with both headings for 2008-2026 samples, no robots.txt on the mirror, public-domain works (KLS). Against (a): sampled not crawled; 2002-2007 use a different page structure; two date formats; USPTO terms discourage bulk access, so cache one request per week-page (KLS). The notes recommend (a).
2. **Cross-check source before OPS activates.** Options: (a) wait for OPS credentials and wire `python-epo-ops-client` `legal()`; (b) manual, documented Google Patents/Espacenet spot checks for the shortlist; (c) no cross-check, downgrade confidence. Evidence: anonymous OPS is 403; activation time undocumented; Google Patents automation violates ToS; INPADOC reinstatements lag months anyway (KLS). Design must define the confidence downgrade when only one source exists (METH).
3. **How far to index OG weeks and what counts as full reinstatement coverage.** Options: 52 weeks only; since 2016 (covers 4/8/12-year lapses of 2010-2022 grants); full 2008→ sweep. Evidence: KLS says a 12-year lookback is unnecessary for the demo; METH's Validation item 4 requires stating coverage and downgrading confidence when later weeks are missing.
4. **Discovery query path and byte budget.** Options: (a) `patents.publications` cpc+title (35.7 GB, grants to 2026-09-15); (b) `patentsview.cpc_current` + `patentsview.patent` (1.8 + 5.9 GB, grants ≤ 2021-09-28); (c) `google_patents_research` `cpc_low` + `title` (35 GB). Evidence: 1 TiB/month sandbox; a full run is ~260 GB (~25%); unclustered tables ignore `WHERE`; cache to `fixtures/`/`outputs/` because tables expire in 60 days (BQ). Decide whether to spend 2.6 GB PEDS lookups per candidate for historical fee events.
5. **ODP adapter scope.** Options: (a) fixture-tested adapter marked "requires ID.me-verified key" with the 2018 layout and `EXP.`/`EXPX` rules; (b) omit; (c) also ingest the 2025-03-07 Wayback zip (180 MB) as a labelled historical fixture source. Evidence: 403 on every path; ID.me via third-party guide and USPTO bulletin (unverified against USPTO's own JS page); three independent codebases agree on the semantics (KLS; GH).
6. **Workflow versus agent, and the number of LLM calls.** Options: (a) five bounded structured-output calls in plain nodes; (b) add one optional `ToolNode` loop gated on probed capability; (c) `create_agent` with middleware. Evidence for (a): Anthropic definitions, 15× multi-agent tokens, correlated same-model failures, "actually execute tools" is satisfied by code nodes (LG; METH). Against (c): fixed, auditable pipeline; `create_agent` suits open-ended chat (LG).
7. **Human-gate mechanics.** Options: `interrupt()` + `Command(goto=...)` with `response_schema` (LG recommended); `HumanInTheLoopMiddleware` (`approve|edit|reject|respond`); blocking `input()`. Evidence: `interrupt()` re-executes the node from the start and needs a checkpointer; `input()` is not restart-safe; `interrupt_before/after` are debug-only (LG). Also decide the approval vocabulary and revision cap (gpt-researcher precedent).
8. **Checkpointer and durability.** Options: `SqliteSaver` (recommended), `InMemorySaver` (tests only), `PostgresSaver`. Evidence: restart survival requires SQLite; `durability="sync"` for the demo (LG).
9. **Structured-output method per model group.** Options: `json_schema` (strict), `function_calling`, `json_mode` + repair; runtime capability probe cached in `stats.json`; route `free-tools` vs `free`. Evidence: OVH gpt-oss lacks function calling; Groq strict mode on four models only and incompatible with streaming/tools; OpenRouter per-endpoint support; freellm's `openai/` prefix bypasses LiteLLM capability tables (LG).
10. **Terminal UI.** Options: Rich + Typer (recommended); Textual (full-screen, `run_test()`/`Pilot`); plain `input()`. Evidence: interrupts pause the graph entirely so Rich's exit-Live-ask-resume pattern suffices; Textual costs workers and async tests; deepagents-code precedent is 52k lines (LG).
11. **HTTP caching library and client.** Options: `hishel` 2.0 simple mode with explicit TTL (GH's updated recommendation) vs `hishel>=1.4,<2` RFC 9111 `Controller`; `httpx` 0.28.1 (no release since 2024-12) vs the `httpx2` fork used by patent-client-agents. Evidence: 2.0 renamed classes and dropped RFC defaults; OG pages set no useful cache headers (GH).
12. **OPS client.** Options: `python-epo-ops-client` 4.2.3 (sync, `requests`, `dogpile`) behind a thread, or ~100 lines of OAuth + header throttling on httpx. Evidence: library maintained, Apache-2.0, `legal()` since 2025-07; still requires own XML parsing; throttle category disagreement (see contradictions) (GH; KLS).
13. **Document generation route.** Options: code-built python-docx/python-pptx from `ReportBundle` with checked-in templates (recommended); Pandoc from Markdown (fallback); docxtpl. Evidence: cross-file equality by construction; python-docx needs four oxml injections; Pandoc lacks native pptx charts and column widths; docxtpl LGPL and run-splitting (DOC). Sub-decisions: include a TOC field (and the `updateFields` dialog) or not; PNG vs native timeline on slide 6.
14. **Validation depth.** Options: round-trip extraction only; plus vendored Anthropic validator; plus `openxml-audit` with allowlist; plus LibreOffice render and page count when present. Evidence: validators pass on python-pptx output and on fixed python-docx output; LibreOffice absent locally and on CI runners (DOC).
15. **Status label enum and conflict rule.** Options for labels: DOC's `Expired (nonpayment) | Reinstated | In grace period | Expired (end of term) | Conflicting evidence | Active (related)` versus GH's `expired_nonpayment | reinstated | in_grace_period | expired_term | conflicting | unknown`. Conflict rule: later-dated official notice wins; record both; label INPADOC/Google as lagging (KLS). Decide how "pending petition" and "end of term with PTA unknown" are labelled.
16. **Evidence grading vocabulary.** Options: Admiralty A-F × 1-6 plus IPCC confidence (METH recommended); ICD 203 seven bands; both. Evidence: no source prescribes the combination; ICD 203 primary unreadable this session; GRADE argues for separating certainty from recommendation (METH).
17. **Powered-device exclusion thresholds.** Options: co-class list only; co-class + independent-claim keyword rule with human review for description-only mentions (DOM proposal); LLM classification with evidence quotes (LG call 3). Evidence: CPC titles label powered groups; 75-85% of core-union grants have no powered co-class; the keyword thresholds are not from literature (DOM).
18. **Related-patent status verification.** Options: verify each related record's status live (OPS budget permitting); emit "status to verify" flags only; hybrid for the shortlist. Evidence: TD and related status are not in BigQuery; OPS gives per-patent lookups at ~60/min (BQ; DOM; KLS).
19. **Opportunity scoring axes and weights.** Options: the brief's four (commercial relevance, engineering effort, evidence quality, unresolved risks) with visible weights in state; bibliometric indicators as inputs not outputs. Evidence: non-renewal is a low-private-value signal; separate "value to former owner" from "value to new entrant" (DOM).
20. **Market-context sourcing.** Options: BLS CE line only (free, primary); add Census NAICS 339994 via a free `CENSUS_API_KEY`; cite paywalled publisher estimates with "unverified" labels or omit. Evidence: Census API now redirects to `missing_key.html`; Prop 65 text not retrieved (DOM).
21. **Observability.** Options: local structlog JSONL + `cost.json` (required); LangSmith optional via env vars. Evidence: 5k free traces/month; sanitised logs are a deliverable (LG).
22. **Process artefacts.** Options: SPEC.md in EARS + four MADR-minimal ADRs (002-005) with Confirmation lines (METH); fewer or more ADRs. Evidence: Nygard's significance test; repo already has `adr-001` in a compatible style (METH).

## Contradictions and open gaps across the notes

- **What "keyless" means differs by note.** KLS calls OPS the cross-check for a "no key" design even though OPS requires registration, OAuth2 credentials and a pending manual activation; GH records the registration as pending; BQ's "keyless" BigQuery still needs a GCP project with ADC and a sandbox quota; DOM finds the Census API now needs a (free) key; `patent-client-agents`' catalogue lists Google Patents as "No credentials required" but KLS and GH both reject it because it scrapes against Google's ToS and robots.txt. Read "keyless" as "no USPTO ODP key", and state each source's actual credential in the data-source docs (KLS; GH; BQ; DOM).
- **Whether ID.me is required for an ODP key.** KLS and GH both cite the third-party `API_KEY_GUIDE.md` (USPTO.gov account → ID.me link at `data.uspto.gov/myodp`) and USPTO's 2025 bulletin naming ID.me as the verification vendor; GH also records an Apify listing claiming that "as of June 18, 2026" only a USPTO.gov account with mandatory MFA is needed, without ID.me.
